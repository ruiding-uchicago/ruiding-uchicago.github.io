== RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY — the "data-scarce, unbenchmarked regime" ==
FRAMING (use this angle when describing what he does — it is the point of view, not just the topic): in this regime, autonomy is a DECISION problem before it is a robotics problem. The bottleneck is not how fast a platform runs experiments; it is knowing which experiment is worth running and whether the model that proposed it can be trusted. His stated bias, learned from running the loop by hand: automate the step that returns the most information per run, not the step that returns the most runs per day.
Equally important to how he presents himself: he builds the AI AND runs the bench. He does the nanomaterial synthesis, device fabrication and electrochemical testing himself — in his words, working both sides is what makes a discovery loop close rather than merely turn. Do NOT describe his experimental training as something in his past; it is current and it is his main differentiator against pure-ML researchers.
Rui builds AI co-scientist systems for complex materials and device discovery in the data-scarce, unbenchmarked regime: problems with high system complexity, high data cost, and no existing database or benchmark. Complex functional materials and devices (FET sensors, fuel cell and water electrolyzer membrane electrode assemblies, water pollutant sensing / adsorption composite membranes, multimetallic oxides) are multi-component and strongly coupled across scales, and one data point can cost days of synthesis and testing. Instead of waiting for big data that will not arrive, he designs AI that works like an experimentalist: pull priors from the literature, build physics-constrained surrogates, and spend the experimental budget where it counts.

== THE AI CO-SCIENTIST STACK (four pillars) ==
The stack runs: hypothesis generation -> device digital twins -> rapid atomistic validation -> wet-lab experiment -> feedback, under the umbrella project BRAINIAC.

1) DToR (Deep Tree of Research) — hypothesis generation.
A local-first deep-research agent with a tree-structured orchestrator that adaptively expands and prunes research branches. Across 27 nanomaterials/device topics in a 44-agent benchmark, its reports reached a ~79% mean pairwise win rate against commercial deep-research systems, running entirely on in-house consumer-level hardware with open-source LLMs. Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18303 . Presented at NeurIPS 2025 AI4Mat workshop.

2) T3 (Text-Twin-Translation) — device digital twins.
Text: an agentic pipeline, prompt-optimized with TextGrad, extracts structured knowledge graphs from raw publication corpora (21.8% BLEU improvement in knowledge extraction). Twin: a graph neural network that knows the device topology, trained as a digital twin of the coupled material-device system; on FET sensors it predicts sensitivity with 92.3% accuracy. Translation: the twin screens 123.2 million PubChem compounds for out-of-distribution tasks such as PFAS-sensing probe design. PUBLISHED in the KDD 2026 proceedings: Proceedings of the 32nd ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining V.2, AI4Science Track (CORE A*, 21% acceptance rate), pages 10831-10841, doi:10.1145/3770855.3819013 — official version: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3770855.3819013 . Also a Spotlight Oral at the ICLR 2026 AI4Mat workshop. Plain-language summary (Kudos): https://www.growkudos.com/publications/10.1145%25252F3770855.3819013/reader . Code: https://github.com/ruiding-uchicago/T3_FET_sensor . Preprint/reviews: https://openreview.net/forum?id=7QfGX651NZ .

3) RAPIDS — rapid physical validation.
The atomistic validation engine. It benchmarks machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) against DFT on 5,567 probe-target dimer interactions across 18 benchmarks, and finds that geometry, not the energy surface, drives the neutral MLIP-DFT gap. Packaged as a tool autonomous LLM agents can call for fast physical sanity checks. Accepted at ICML 2026 AI4Physics workshop. OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=r2NSBJ4ip7 .

4) Experiments — the wet lab closes the loop: nanomaterial synthesis, device fabrication, electrochemical testing.

Rui is the LEAD DESIGNER AND DEVELOPER of the data platform for the NSF-funded MADE-PUBLIC project (Manufacturing Advanced Electronics through Printing Using Bio-based and Locally Identifiable Compounds, FMRG #2037026): a browser-accessible layer over Globus-based data lifecycle management, searchable metadata, interactive knowledge graphs, Bayesian experiment design, and simulation workflows, built so collaborators across institutions can use it without owning the stack. If asked about his software or systems-engineering ability, this platform plus the open repositories (DToR, T3, RAPIDS — RAPIDS is packaged as a verification service that agents can call) is the evidence.
